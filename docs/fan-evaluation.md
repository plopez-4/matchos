# Five-fan comprehension check

Status: prepared; no participant sessions have been conducted.

Purpose: determine whether a returning fan understands the contribution and its timing, rather than only liking the animation or commentator voice. This small study cannot establish engagement uplift or general accuracy.

## Setup

Use the branded main fan page at `/`, not the development inspector. Check that AI-written text is labeled as such; a configured connection alone is not proof a particular request returned AI prose. Record whether the actual paragraph used AI or fallback.

Use one frozen evaluation version and save the actual displayed recap, its prompt hash, audience, language and supporting events for every trial. Record whether it was AI prose or fallback. Do not mix versions during the five-person study.

Primary trial: use a fresh Palace–Newcastle replay and pause around 62:30, after Sarr's equalizer at 60:11 and before the 66:11 goal. Confirm the evidence shows Tomiyasu's recovery, three completed team passes and Sarr's goal-producing shot. Do not show this expected answer to participants.

Use the actual recorded actors: some seeded matchdays swap participants and scorer. Event evidence, not this example or a remembered name, is the answer key.

Second situation, if testing both perspectives: pause the featured United–City match around 20:00–25:00. Confirm the actual selected creator/shooter contribution and whether its other attempts came after the 15:11 goal. This tests whether fans distinguish a recurring pattern from that goal's build-up. If a different pattern is selected, score against its actual evidence.

Recruit five football fans. Use anonymous participant IDs only. Give three Casual first and two Analyst first, then reverse the order on a different situation if time permits. Do not explain the expected insight, correct them during a trial, or teach where the evidence button is.

Say: “You have just returned to this match. Use this screen to understand what you missed, then continue however you would normally.” Observe the first action and time taken. Allow them to replay or listen if they choose.

## Ask after their first attempt

1. What is the current score, and who scored?
2. Which player or combination stood out in the recap, and what did they do?
3. Describe the order of the goal and the other attempts mentioned.
4. What would you inspect or do next? Ask them to show it.
5. Which words helped, repeated something, or gave you an impression the evidence did not support?

Also ask whether they would choose to watch the replay now and why. Record their explanation; do not count saying yes as demonstrated engagement. Observe whether they can find and open the named action without coaching.

For Analyst, additionally ask what qualifies as a recorded pass-to-shot link and what the count cannot establish. Do not expect Casual to recite a technical definition.

## Record and decide

Use `fan-evaluation-results.csv`. Leave unknown observations blank. Separate a misunderstanding caused by wording from a failure to find the next action. Preserve a short exact quote with consent, or paraphrase and label it as a paraphrase. Do not report uncollected answers as successful trials.

Proposed small-study gate: at least four of five correctly explain the named contribution and its chronology without coaching, and can find their next action. Any repeated misconception that later attempts caused an earlier goal requires correction even if the overall score passes. Compare whether Analyst adds understanding of the rule rather than just more reading.

This is an editorial correction milestone. No new tracking, dashboard or narrator category follows automatically from the result.
